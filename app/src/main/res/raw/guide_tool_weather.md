Use the Weather tool to roughly predict the weather without an Internet connection.

**Warning:** The weather prediction is a best guess from the available sensor data, and it might not be accurate. If Trail Sense predicts clear weather but you see what look like storm clouds rolling in, trust your instincts. Storms can roll in with gradual changes in pressure. Seek shelter if you sense a storm approaching, regardless of what Trail Sense predicts.

## Read the weather prediction
The weather prediction is shown at the top of the screen. It doesn't use the Internet. It uses the pressure history, historic temperatures (the 30-year average), and the clouds from the Clouds tool.

An estimate of when the weather will change is shown below the prediction. The accuracy of the time frame depends on whether the clouds indicate a weather front. If a front is detected, an approximate time is shown. Otherwise, the estimate is vaguer, such as "soon" or "later".

To change how sensitive the prediction is, set **Settings > Weather > Forecast sensitivity**. Higher sensitivity can detect more patterns but might produce more false predictions.

## Record pressure history
If the weather monitor is on, it records the pressure history automatically. Otherwise, the pressure updates only when you open the Weather tool. For accuracy, record the pressure every 30 minutes.

## Use the weather monitor
The weather monitor runs in the background to record the pressure history.

### Start and stop the weather monitor
To start the weather monitor, do one of the following:

- Tap the start icon in the bottom-right corner.
- Enable **Settings > Weather > Monitor weather**.

A notification shows the current prediction and has an option to stop the weather monitor.

To stop the weather monitor, tap the stop icon in the bottom-right corner, or use the option in the notification.

### Change the recording frequency
By default, the weather monitor records every 30 minutes. To change the interval, do one of the following:

- Tap the time under the weather monitor label in the bottom-left corner, and enter a new interval.
- Set **Settings > Weather > Weather update frequency**.

### Improve the accuracy
To improve the accuracy, grant Trail Sense the Location permission. Trail Sense then determines your elevation with the GPS, which helps it convert the pressure to sea level pressure and improves the prediction.

### Customize the notification
In **Settings > Weather**, choose what the notification shows:

- **Pressure**: The current pressure.
- **Temperature**: The current temperature. For more information, see the Thermometer settings section of the Settings guide.

The weather monitor and daily weather notifications show a large, colored icon for the prediction. To turn this off, disable **Settings > Weather > Show colored notification icon**.

To turn off weather monitor notifications, use Android's notification settings for Trail Sense.

### Get a daily weather notification
To get a daily weather notification, do the following:

1. Start the weather monitor. You don't receive the daily notification while the weather monitor is off.
2. Enable **Settings > Weather > Daily weather notification**.
3. Optional: Set the time of the notification in **Settings > Weather > Daily weather time**.

## Get storm alerts
If the weather monitor is on, Trail Sense can notify you of storms. To turn on storm alerts, do the following:

1. Enable **Settings > Weather > Storm alerts**.
2. Optional: Set **Settings > Weather > Storm sensitivity**. Higher sensitivity might trigger false alerts, and lower sensitivity might miss some storms.
3. Optional: Enable **Settings > Weather > Use alarm for storm alert** to play the alert on the alarm audio channel, which sounds even if media and notifications are muted.
4. Optional: Enable **Settings > Weather > Mute alarm at night** to use the alarm only during the day.

## View weather details
The Weather tool also shows more information about the current and predicted weather.

### Alerts
The Weather tool shows alerts when it detects a storm or when historic temperatures suggest hot or cold conditions.

### Pressure
A pressure history chart is shown at the top of the screen. The chart shows the following:

- **Falling pressure**: A storm might be approaching.
- **Rising pressure**: The weather is likely clearing.
- **Steady pressure**: No significant changes are expected.

The current pressure and its tendency (the change over time) are listed below the chart.

The current pressure system is also shown. Low pressure often means poor weather, and high pressure means fair conditions. To see a description of the pressure system, tap it.

To change the history duration, set **Settings > Weather > Pressure history**.

### Temperature
The weather list shows the current temperature, from historic data or the onboard thermometer. It also shows the daily high and low temperatures from historic data. You can do the following:

- To see a temperature history chart, tap the current temperature.
- To see a 24-hour temperature prediction chart based on historic temperatures, tap the high and low temperatures.

### Humidity
If your device has a humidity sensor, the current humidity is shown. To see a humidity history chart, tap it.

### Clouds
The last logged cloud type appears in the weather list. To see a description of the cloud type, tap it.

### Weather front
The current weather front appears in the weather list. Fronts typically indicate weather changes and are often associated with precipitation and wind. To see a description of the weather front, tap it.

### Last updated
The time of the last weather update appears in the weather list.

## Export weather data
To export the recorded weather data to a CSV file, use **Settings > Weather > Export weather readings**.

## Understand the accuracy of historic temperatures
Climate normals (historic temperatures) are based on the 30-year average of the historic data and show what the weather is usually like. Actual values can differ for many reasons but should stay fairly close to the historic values. Large differences are usually caused by a storm or other weather event.

Historic temperatures are estimated with a custom 30-year "climate normal" model derived from the NASA Global Modeling and Assimilation Office MERRA-2 data (1991 - 2020).

## Quick action
To use the Weather Monitor quick action, enable it in the settings for the tab you want it on. Tap the quick action button to turn the weather monitor on or off.

## Widgets
You can place the following widgets on your device's home screen or view them in the app:

- **Weather**: Shows the current weather and temperature prediction.
- **Pressure**: Shows the current pressure and tendency arrow.
- **Pressure chart**: Shows the pressure chart.
